This website was built entirely with AI

I'm a designer, not an engineer, and I had never coded a website before. I designed this site in Figma, then built all of it in a single day by working with AI (Claude). Every line of code was written that way. My job was the one I do on any team: setting direction, making the design calls, reviewing the work, and deciding what to keep and what to cut.

  1. From Figma to code. The AI read my Figma frame directly and turned it into HTML and CSS. I reviewed it against my design and asked for changes, the same way I'd work with an engineer.
  2. Getting it live. I learned how files, folders and image paths work by fixing images that didn't show up, then put the code on GitHub and deployed it on Vercel. When the live homepage showed an old version, I tracked down why and fixed it.
  3. A resume that works for people and software. I built an A4 resume, tested that applicant tracking systems read it cleanly, and put it on its own page with a download button that stays pinned and shrinks as you scroll.
  4. Redesigning with principles. I asked for a redesign grounded in design principles, then shaped it: a checkout-inspired system with one accent color, a clear type scale and an 8px spacing grid. I tried different fonts and tightened the project cards so two fit on screen without scrolling.
  5. Adding interaction, then editing it. I added a tap-through prototype of the pricing portal and a copy-email button. I also tried a pricing toggle and project filters, and removed them when they crowded the page. Cutting was as important as adding.
  6. Making it accessible. I audited every page against WCAG 2.2 AA and fixed what it found, from color contrast to keyboard focus and screen reader labels.
  7. Protecting client work. My case studies include work under client confidentiality, so they sit behind a password that's checked on the server. The rest of the site stays public for recruiters.
  8. Case studies and my own domain. I turned my notes and past presentation into case study pages with a pinned section menu, and moved my domain, vagishasingh.com, from Wix to Vercel.

Built to be accessible

I wanted this site to work for everyone who visits, however they browse. So I treated accessibility as part of the design, not a final check.

  • Meets WCAG 2.2 AA in automated testing. Every page passes an axe-core audit with zero issues, on desktop and mobile.
  • Works with just a keyboard. Every link, button and prototype control can be reached with Tab, with a visible focus ring and a "Skip to content" link.
  • Makes sense to screen readers. Clear headings and page regions, descriptive labels, and spoken updates as you tap through the prototype.
  • Readable contrast. All text meets AA contrast ratios, including small labels.
  • Holds up when zoomed. Pages reflow down to 320px wide, so nothing breaks at 400% zoom.
  • Respects motion and contrast settings. Animations turn off if you've asked your device to reduce motion, and controls stay visible in high contrast mode.
