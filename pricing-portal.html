Making sense of pricing in-store

In carrier stores, Apple’s Digital Catalog hid real savings, so iPhones felt more expensive than they were. I redesigned how offers are signaled and structured, so customers see affordability from the first screen.

Overview

At carrier stores like Verizon and AT&T, shoppers explore iPhones on Apple’s Digital Catalog. As promotions grew more complex, the catalog didn’t keep up, so iPhones looked more expensive than they really were.

The system

Partners enter all pricing and offers in a backend Pricing Portal that Apple doesn’t control. Apple can only shape how that content is presented in-store.

The problem

  • Every offer looked the same. Installments, trade-ins, discounts and bundles shared one card template.
  • Key info got skipped. “Reasons to Buy” read like legal fine print.
  • Nothing signaled offers existed until shoppers reached pricing.
83%of smartphone shoppers left without buying, mostly “waiting for a better deal” or needing “more time to research.”
#1most requested info in-store: promotions and special offers.

Constraint & goals

I couldn’t change the content, so I changed how it reads.

Character limits, regional legal rules and global scale all applied. Within that, I set three goals:

  • 1. AwarenessShow that offers exist before anyone goes looking.
  • 2. VisibilityMake offers stand out on the Pricing Page.
  • 3. FlexibilitySupport every offer type within existing templates.

The solution

Separate cost from value

Price stays anchored on the page. Offers get their own space, with a shared visual language.

A persistent offers entry point

It flashes blue to catch the eye, then settles to grey. It reuses Apple’s existing bar patterns, so there’s nothing new to learn.

Offers stay in view at the point of decision

The same entry point sits on the Pricing Page and opens a modal, so offers stay visible while shoppers compare costs.

Edge cases covered

  • No offer entered: the familiar “refer to store” card shows.
  • Offers on only some configurations: other offers fill the space.
  • France: the required DAS overlay appears on page load.

How I got there

I tested how much visibility offers needed, from a subtle ribbon to elevated “featured deals” with a distinct template per offer type, then landed on the lightest signal that still drives awareness.
